# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith. Criticise code, not people.

## Our standards

Behaviour that builds a good project:

- Giving and receiving feedback gracefully — especially in code review, where the
  [comment taxonomy](docs/sdlc/04-code-review-standard.md#comment-taxonomy) exists precisely so
  that "I disagree" is unambiguous and unthreatening
- Accepting that you will sometimes be wrong, and saying so without ceremony
- Respecting that people have different levels of experience; nobody arrives knowing this
- Focusing on what is best for the project rather than on being right

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Personal attacks, insults, or derogatory comments
- Harassment of any kind, public or private
- Publishing others' private information without permission
- Sustained disruption of discussions
- Condescension dressed as technical rigour

## Review, specifically

Code review is where a project's culture is most visible, and where it most often goes wrong.

Review comments address **the code**. "This function does three things" is a review comment.
"You always write functions that do three things" is not — it is about a person, and it makes
the next review harder for everyone.

As an author, review feedback is about your code, not about you. As a reviewer, remember
someone spent real effort on what you are reading, and that being right is not the same as
being useful.

## Enforcement

Report concerns to the repository owner via a private message or a
[private security advisory](https://github.com/victorneves-plank/sdlc-harness-todo/security/advisories/new)
if no other private channel is available. Reports are handled confidentially.

Maintainers may edit, remove or reject contributions, and may ban contributors, for behaviour
they consider inappropriate.
